# Profiles: Launch Vehicles

This document contains historical profiles, design contexts, and narrative details for the Earth-based launch vehicles used to transport payloads to the Moon.

---

## Saturn V

*   **Operator:** NASA (United States)
*   **Designer:** Wernher von Braun / Marshall Space Flight Center
*   **Operational Era:** 1967–1973
*   **Narrative Profile:** 
    The Saturn V remains the iconic symbol of human space exploration. Developed to support the Apollo program, it stands 111 meters tall and generated 34.5 million Newtons of thrust at launch. It successfully completed 13 launches with zero cargo or crew losses, sending humans to the Moon for the first time.
*   **In-Game Concept:** High-cost, high-reliability heavy-lifter. Best reserved for launching primary habitation hubs where loss of payload is not an option.

---

## N1 Rocket

*   **Operator:** Soviet Space Program (USSR)
*   **Designer:** Sergei Korolev / Vasily Mishin (OKB-1)
*   **Operational Era:** 1969–1972
*   **Narrative Profile:** 
    Designed as the Soviet counterpart to the Saturn V for the crewed lunar landing program. The N1 used a complex array of 30 small NK-15 engines on its first stage. Plagued by plumbing complications and control systems failures, all four test flights ended in catastrophic explosions before first-stage separation.
*   **In-Game Concept:** High payload capability, but has a high failure probability. High-risk, high-reward launch model.

## Proton-K

*   **Operator:** Soviet Union / Russia (Roscosmos)
*   **Designer:** Vladimir Chelomey (OKB-52)
*   **Operational Era:** 1965–2012
*   **Narrative Profile:** 
    A heavy-duty workhorse of the Soviet/Russian fleet. The Proton-K launched all of the Salyut space stations, the Mir core modules, and the Zvezda module for the ISS. It was also used to launch the robotic Luna sample return missions.
*   **In-Game Concept:** Reliable, budget-friendly medium transport suited for routine probe deployment.

---

## Long March 5

*   **Operator:** CNSA (China)
*   **Designer:** China Academy of Launch Vehicle Technology (CALT)
*   **Operational Era:** 2016–Present
*   **Narrative Profile:** 
    Representing China's generation of heavy cryogenic rockets. The Long March 5 is the booster behind China's modern lunar exploration successes, including the Chang'e 5 sample return and Chang'e 6 far-side sample return missions.
*   **In-Game Concept:** A balanced governmental lifter with a solid payload capacity and moderate cost.

---

## Falcon Heavy

*   **Operator:** SpaceX (United States)
*   **Designer:** Elon Musk / SpaceX Engineering Team
*   **Operational Era:** 2018–Present
*   **Narrative Profile:** 
    Composed of three unified Falcon 9 cores. The Falcon Heavy introduced commercial reusability to heavy-lift spaceflight, dramatically reducing costs per kilogram to orbit. It is slated to launch elements of NASA’s Gateway station into lunar orbit.
*   **In-Game Concept:** The entry point for commercial space operations. Low base cost due to booster reuse dynamics.

---

## SLS Block 1

*   **Operator:** NASA (United States)
*   **Designer:** Boeing, Aerojet Rocketdyne, Northrop Grumman
*   **Operational Era:** 2022–Present
*   **Narrative Profile:** 
    The core launch vehicle of NASA's Artemis program. The Space Launch System (SLS) leverages Space Shuttle heritage (RS-25 engines and solid rocket boosters) to deliver the crewed Orion spacecraft to Trans-Lunar Injection.
*   **In-Game Concept:** State-backed exploration platform with exceptional lift metrics but high operational costs.
